# Corporate/M&A Methodology — RankPilot RAG v1

## Purpose

This document defines how RankPilot should understand and apply Corporate/M&A ranking logic across major legal directories.

Corporate/M&A must not be treated as a generic transactional practice. RankPilot should evaluate each matter according to its strategic relevance, client profile, deal structure, firm role, execution complexity, lawyer leadership and market impact.

## Core definition

Corporate/M&A covers:

- acquisitions
- disposals
- mergers
- joint ventures
- capitalisations
- entity formation
- operating agreements
- shareholder agreements
- governance matters
- corporate restructurings
- transactional reorganisations
- strategic commercial expansion
- related financing arrangements tied to a transaction

The same matter may appear across multiple practice areas if the editorial focus changes materially.

## Core editorial principle

Corporate/M&A rankings do not reward volume alone.

RankPilot must prioritise:

- strategic relevance
- sophistication
- visibility
- market perception
- execution complexity
- client prestige
- lawyer leadership
- competitive impact

## Primary Corporate/M&A lens

Corporate/M&A analysis focuses on:

- strategic transaction execution
- buyer/seller representation
- negotiation complexity
- transactional architecture
- governance implications
- expansion strategy
- market positioning
- execution sophistication

## RankPilot must identify

Before evaluating a Corporate/M&A matter, RankPilot must identify:

1. Transaction type
2. Client side
3. Firm role
4. Deal value or value band
5. Strategic purpose
6. Sector relevance
7. Jurisdictions involved
8. Regulatory or governance complexity
9. Negotiation difficulty
10. Lawyer leadership
11. Market impact
12. Directory fit

## High-ranking Corporate/M&A signals

Strong matters often include:

- economically significant transaction
- visible or strategic client
- sophisticated counterparties
- strategic market expansion
- regulated or technically complex industry
- execution risk
- possibility of deal failure
- regulatory uncertainty
- institutional coordination
- sophisticated negotiation structure
- high-level legal engineering
- large or highly specialised legal team
- visible star partners
- senior associate coordination

## Lower-ranking / routine deal indicators

RankPilot should treat the following as weaker signals unless compensated by strategic complexity:

- low value
- limited execution risk
- straightforward due diligence
- simple structure
- short execution timeline
- limited negotiation complexity
- small legal team
- low regulatory exposure
- little strategic market impact
- generic role description

## Deal value rule

Deal value alone should never determine sophistication.

A mid-market matter can be highly rankable if it involves:

- sophisticated regulatory issues
- strategic market entry
- startup licensing complexity
- regulated industries
- restructuring or distressed acquisition
- auction process
- high execution risk
- institutional negotiations
- complex governance structure
- sophisticated financing architecture

RankPilot must prioritise complexity narrative over deal size narrative when appropriate.

## Cross-border rule

Cross-border capability is an important sophistication signal, but sophistication must be evaluated relative to market context.

A domestic deal may still be highly sophisticated if it involves:

- difficult regulatory environment
- political complexity
- institutional negotiation
- fragmented ownership structure
- distressed assets
- reorganisations
- market entry barriers
- innovative structures
- unclear regulation
- difficult execution environment

## Individual ranking layer

Corporate/M&A is not only an institutional ranking exercise.

RankPilot must identify:

- lead partner
- relationship partner
- execution partner
- strategic negotiators
- supporting senior lawyers
- next-generation talent

Weak signal:

“The firm advised…”

Strong signal:

“Partner X led negotiations for the seller, supported by Partner Y on governance and Senior Associate Z on closing coordination.”

## Firm archetype model

### High-End Firm

Characteristics:

- star partners
- elite market perception
- premium institutional clients
- highly sophisticated matters
- high-value transactions
- strong succession generation
- strategic leadership
- market-defining work

### Strong Mid-Market Firm

Characteristics:

- ex-elite lawyers
- sophisticated but lower-ticket matters
- agile structures
- strong growth potential
- flexible pricing
- strategic niche positioning

### Emerging Boutique

Characteristics:

- specialist ex-biglaw founders
- focused premium expertise
- single-practice dominance
- strong market momentum
- high technical sophistication in narrow fields

### Corporate Generalist

Characteristics:

- broad service capability
- weaker differentiation
- mixed client base
- lower strategic positioning
- tangential deal roles
- lower visibility
- limited market leadership

## Strategic trajectory model

### Entry strategy

Prioritise:

- strongest matters only
- recognisable clients
- credibility
- differentiation
- visible leadership

### Promotion strategy

Prioritise:

- evolution from previous years
- sophistication increase
- stronger market position
- better matters
- stronger lawyer visibility

### Consolidation strategy

Prioritise:

- consistency
- continuity
- repeat client trust
- sustained sophistication
- stable leadership

### Recovery strategy

Prioritise:

- retained capability
- retained clients
- rebuilding narrative
- resilience
- strategic repositioning

## Competitive shift events

RankPilot must identify:

- lateral hires
- team acquisitions
- mergers
- boutique integrations
- strategic expansions
- office launches
- partner migrations

These are not simple “team updates.”

They are competitive repositioning events.

The submission should connect them to:

- matters
- client wins
- practice expansion
- sector strength
- market perception
- new capabilities

## Confidential matter rules

If client name, value, counterparties or advisers cannot be disclosed, the submission must compensate with:

- industry significance
- client profile description
- approximate value band
- strategic importance
- complexity explanation
- market impact
- execution challenges
- role description
- lawyer leadership visibility

Confidentiality cannot eliminate context.

## Common submission failures

RankPilot should detect:

- lack of differentiation
- fragmented internal information
- political/internal bias in matter selection
- weak documentation
- no strategic positioning
- no visibility strategy
- generic matter descriptions
- collections of matters without narrative
- overreliance on volume
- weak individual lawyer visibility
- role ambiguity
- excessive adjectives without evidence

## RankPilot instruction

RankPilot should not behave like:

- a summarizer
- a drafting assistant
- a generic legal AI

It should behave like a strategic editorial ranking intelligence engine.

The objective is to operationalize expert editorial judgment into reusable contextual logic.
