# Chambers Banking Scoring Rubric — RankPilot RAG v1

## Purpose

This rubric gives RankPilot a structured way to score Banking & Finance matters for Chambers.

The score is not a prediction of ranking outcome. It is an internal diagnostic tool to determine the strength of a matter as evidence in a Chambers submission.

Maximum score: 100 points.

---

## 1. Scoring categories

### 1. Correct table fit — 10 points

Evaluates whether the matter belongs in the relevant Chambers Banking table.

10 = Clearly belongs in the exact table.
7 = Mostly relevant, with minor overlap.
4 = Banking-adjacent but may belong elsewhere.
0 = Wrong table.

Red flags:

- project finance in Banking where separate Project Finance exists
- capital markets work in Banking where separate Capital Markets exists
- regulatory work presented as transactional finance
- asset finance submitted to general Banking when a dedicated Asset Finance table exists

---

### 2. Transaction significance — 10 points

Evaluates size, strategic importance and market relevance.

10 = Major, market-significant, high-value or strategically important matter.
7 = Strong matter with good commercial relevance.
4 = Useful but ordinary transaction.
0 = Minor or routine matter.

Indicators:

- big-ticket value
- major client
- market visibility
- connection to important M&A, infrastructure or restructuring event
- strategically important sector

---

### 3. Structural complexity — 15 points

Evaluates financial sophistication.

15 = Highly complex structure.
11 = Strong complexity.
7 = Some complexity.
3 = Limited complexity.
0 = No visible complexity.

Examples of complexity:

- multi-tranche facility
- syndicated loan
- cross-border security
- mezzanine or subordinated debt
- intercreditor arrangements
- NAV or hybrid fund finance
- sustainability-linked margin ratchet
- asset-based lending mechanics
- complex refinancing or restructuring

---

### 4. Legal sophistication — 10 points

Evaluates legal difficulty beyond ordinary execution.

10 = High legal sophistication.
7 = Clear legal issues.
4 = Some legal complexity.
0 = Routine documentation.

Indicators:

- regulatory complexity
- insolvency overlay
- tax-sensitive structuring
- multi-jurisdictional enforceability
- novel documentation
- complex covenant package
- Sharia-compliant requirements

---

### 5. Firm role strength — 15 points

Evaluates whether the firm had a central role.

15 = Lead counsel / strategic structuring counsel.
11 = Major counsel with substantive responsibility.
7 = Important but limited role.
3 = Local or supporting counsel.
0 = Role unclear or negligible.

RankPilot should penalise matters where the role is vague.

Strong language:

- acted as lead counsel
- advised the lenders
- represented the borrower in structuring and negotiating
- coordinated multi-jurisdictional advice
- led negotiations with the lender syndicate

Weak language:

- assisted with
- advised on certain aspects
- provided local law advice
- supported the transaction
- reviewed documents

---

### 6. Client prestige and relevance — 10 points

Evaluates the importance of the client.

10 = Top-tier financial institution, sponsor, sovereign, DFI, major corporate or market-leading fund.
7 = Recognised market participant.
4 = Mid-market client.
0 = Client not identified or low relevance.

Stronger clients:

- global banks
- development finance institutions
- export credit agencies
- private equity sponsors
- alternative lenders
- listed corporates
- sovereign entities
- major funds

---

### 7. Cross-border / jurisdictional complexity — 10 points

10 = Multiple jurisdictions with substantive legal complexity.
7 = Cross-border element clearly relevant.
4 = Limited foreign element.
0 = purely domestic with no complexity.

Indicators:

- foreign-law financing documents
- cross-border collateral
- offshore SPVs
- multi-jurisdictional security
- international lender syndicate
- foreign borrower or sponsor
- regulatory approvals in multiple jurisdictions

---

### 8. Innovation / novelty — 10 points

10 = Novel, first-of-kind, market-leading or structurally innovative.
7 = Clearly sophisticated or uncommon.
4 = Some innovation.
0 = standard transaction.

Examples:

- first sustainability-linked facility of its type
- innovative NAV structure
- novel security package
- market-first financing
- new private credit structure
- unusual hybrid bank/bond structure

---

### 9. Narrative strength — 10 points

Evaluates whether the matter is written in a way that helps Chambers understand the ranking value.

10 = Clear, persuasive and strategically framed.
7 = Good but could be stronger.
4 = Basic narrative.
0 = unclear or generic.

Strong narrative includes:

- what the transaction was
- who the firm acted for
- why it mattered
- what was complex
- what the firm specifically did
- market impact
- why it belongs in Banking

---

## 2. Final classification

### 85-100: Chambers flagship matter

Use near the top of submission. Strong candidate for editorial narrative and partner positioning.

### 70-84: Strong Chambers matter

Useful evidence of ranking strength. Should be included if well written.

### 55-69: Good but underdeveloped matter

Potentially useful, but requires narrative improvement or additional context.

### 40-54: Weak matter for Chambers

May be included only if needed for volume or client relationship evidence.

### 0-39: Low-value or wrong-fit matter

Do not include unless strategically necessary.

---

## 3. Chambers-specific weighting guidance

For Chambers, RankPilot should weigh especially heavily:

- correct category fit
- role strength
- structural complexity
- client feedback potential
- market significance
- lender-side prestige where relevant
- cross-border sophistication
- partner-level leadership

Chambers is not merely counting transactions. A smaller but highly sophisticated or market-significant matter may be stronger than a larger but routine transaction.
