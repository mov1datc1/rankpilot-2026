# Strong Corporate/M&A Matters — RankPilot RAG v1

## Purpose

This document gives RankPilot examples of strong Corporate/M&A matters.

These are model examples. They are not real client matters.

## Strong Matter 1 — Strategic cross-border acquisition

### Example

The firm acted as lead counsel to a regional market leader in its acquisition of a majority stake in a competitor operating across three jurisdictions. The transaction required coordination of due diligence, negotiation of the share purchase agreement, antitrust analysis, regulatory approvals and a complex post-closing governance structure. The deal strengthened the client’s regional market position and expanded its operations into two new countries.

### Why this is strong

- lead counsel role
- strategic acquisition
- cross-border coordination
- governance complexity
- market expansion
- competitive impact

### Ranking signals

- sophistication
- market visibility
- client prestige
- transactional leadership

## Strong Matter 2 — Private equity buyout

### Example

The firm represented a private equity sponsor in the acquisition of a controlling stake in a healthcare services company. The transaction involved a competitive auction process, leveraged acquisition financing, management rollover arrangements, minority shareholder negotiations and regulatory approvals in a highly regulated sector.

### Why this is strong

- sponsor-side private equity
- auction process
- acquisition financing
- management rollover
- regulated industry
- negotiation complexity

### Ranking signals

- PE sophistication
- execution complexity
- high-quality client work
- strategic role

## Strong Matter 3 — Complex joint venture

### Example

The firm advised an international infrastructure operator on the creation of a joint venture with a local strategic partner to develop and operate logistics assets. The team negotiated governance rights, capital contribution obligations, deadlock mechanisms, exit rights and reserved matters, while coordinating regulatory and corporate approvals.

### Why this is strong

- strategic JV
- governance complexity
- infrastructure sector
- international/local partner dynamics
- long-term commercial relevance

### Ranking signals

- corporate architecture
- governance sophistication
- market entry
- sector relevance

## Strong Matter 4 — Distressed M&A

### Example

The firm advised the buyer in the acquisition of distressed operating assets from a company facing insolvency risk. The transaction required accelerated due diligence, creditor negotiations, regulatory approvals and structuring to isolate legacy liabilities. The team coordinated corporate, insolvency, tax and employment advice to complete the transaction within a compressed timetable.

### Why this is strong

- distressed transaction
- execution risk
- multi-disciplinary complexity
- accelerated timetable
- liability structuring

### Ranking signals

- strategic problem-solving
- execution under pressure
- cross-practice leadership
- sophisticated structuring

## Strong Matter 5 — Strategic carve-out

### Example

The firm represented a multinational company in the carve-out and sale of a non-core business unit. The transaction involved separation of assets, transitional services arrangements, employee transfer issues, IP licensing, commercial contract migration and negotiation of post-closing indemnities.

### Why this is strong

- carve-out complexity
- multinational client
- transitional services
- operational separation
- cross-practice coordination

### Ranking signals

- sophisticated corporate execution
- strategic divestment
- institutional client trust

## Strong Matter 6 — Minority investment with governance rights

### Example

The firm advised a growth-stage technology company on a minority investment by an institutional investor. The matter involved negotiation of preferred rights, investor protections, veto rights, board appointment rights, information rights, anti-dilution provisions and founder control protections.

### Why this is strong

- governance-heavy transaction
- investor/founder alignment
- growth company relevance
- complex shareholder rights

### Ranking signals

- strategic corporate advice
- governance sophistication
- emerging company capability

## Strong Matter 7 — Merger of regulated entities

### Example

The firm acted for one of the merging parties in the combination of two regulated financial services companies. The transaction involved shareholder approvals, regulatory filings, integration planning, board governance issues and negotiation of risk allocation provisions. The matter was strategically significant due to its impact on the client’s market share and service offering.

### Why this is strong

- regulated sector
- merger structure
- governance and approvals
- market impact
- financial services relevance

### Ranking signals

- complex M&A
- regulatory sophistication
- strategic market positioning

## Strong Matter 8 — Family-owned business succession transaction

### Example

The firm advised the controlling shareholders of a family-owned industrial group on the sale of a majority stake to a strategic investor. The transaction required negotiation among family shareholders, governance redesign, management continuity arrangements and phased consideration mechanics. The matter was significant due to the client’s longstanding market position and the complexity of aligning family, management and investor interests.

### Why this is strong

- ownership complexity
- governance redesign
- strategic investor
- founder/family dynamics
- phased consideration

### Ranking signals

- negotiation sophistication
- governance expertise
- strategic advisory role

## Strong matters usually include

- clear transaction type
- client side
- firm role
- strategic purpose
- deal value or relative significance
- complexity
- negotiation challenges
- lawyer leadership
- market or client impact
- correct directory framing
