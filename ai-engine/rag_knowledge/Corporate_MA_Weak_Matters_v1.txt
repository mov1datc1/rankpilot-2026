# Weak Corporate/M&A Matters — RankPilot RAG v1

## Purpose

This document helps RankPilot identify Corporate/M&A matters that are weak, underdeveloped or wrongly positioned.

A weak matter is not always a bad deal. Often, it is a strong transaction written with insufficient context.

## Weak Matter 1 — Generic acquisition

### Example

The firm advised Company X on the acquisition of Company Y.

### Why this is weak

- no deal value
- no client profile
- no firm role detail
- no strategic purpose
- no complexity
- no negotiation issues
- no lawyer visibility

### Required information

- Was the firm buyer or seller counsel?
- Was it lead counsel?
- What was the value?
- Why was the acquisition strategic?
- Was there regulatory complexity?
- Were there multiple jurisdictions?
- Who led the matter?

## Weak Matter 2 — Deal list without narrative

### Example

The firm advised on several acquisitions, disposals and joint ventures during the year.

### Why this is weak

Volume alone does not demonstrate ranking strength.

### RankPilot diagnosis

This may support consistency, but the submission needs standout matters with strategic framing.

## Weak Matter 3 — Entity formation only

### Example

The firm incorporated a new subsidiary for a client.

### Why this is weak

Entity formation is usually routine unless tied to a broader strategic structure.

### Required information

- Was this part of a market entry?
- Was the structure regulated?
- Did it involve foreign investment?
- Was there a complex governance structure?
- Was it part of a JV, fund platform or acquisition?

## Weak Matter 4 — Local counsel only

### Example

The firm acted as local counsel in a cross-border acquisition.

### Why this may be weak

Local counsel roles need explanation to show ranking value.

### Required information

- Did the firm handle local regulatory approvals?
- Did local law issues affect deal structure?
- Was local counsel responsible for closing?
- Did the firm negotiate key local documents?
- Was the local jurisdiction critical to the transaction?

## Weak Matter 5 — Confidential matter with no context

### Example

The firm advised a confidential client on a confidential transaction.

### Why this is weak

Confidentiality cannot eliminate context.

### Better approach

Describe:

- client profile
- industry
- value band
- strategic purpose
- transaction type
- complexity
- firm role
- lawyer leadership

## Weak Matter 6 — Commercial contract misframed as M&A

### Example

The firm advised a client on a distribution agreement.

### Why this may be weak

This may belong in Commercial Contracts rather than Corporate/M&A unless it was part of a strategic transaction or expansion.

## Weak Matter 7 — Financing-heavy matter

### Example

The firm advised the client on financing related to an acquisition.

### Why this may be wrong fit

If the core work was debt structure, lender negotiation, collateral or syndicated finance, the matter may belong in Banking & Finance.

### Corporate/M&A use only if

The firm also handled:

- acquisition documentation
- transaction negotiation
- shareholder arrangements
- closing structure
- buyer/seller strategy

## Weak Matter 8 — Tax-led reorganisation

### Example

The firm advised on a corporate reorganisation.

### Why this may be weak for Corporate/M&A

If the central value was tax structuring, this may belong in Tax.

### Corporate/M&A use only if

The matter involved:

- transaction architecture
- corporate governance
- ownership restructuring
- strategic business transformation
- merger or carve-out elements

## Weak Matter 9 — Unsupported adjectives

### Example

The firm advised on a landmark, complex and highly strategic acquisition.

### Why this is weak

The description claims importance but does not prove it.

### Better approach

Show why:

- first market entry by client
- acquisition of regulated asset
- involved antitrust approval
- required negotiation with multiple shareholders
- included complex earn-out and governance mechanics

## Weak Matter 10 — No lawyer visibility

### Example

The firm advised the client on the transaction.

### Why this is weak

Corporate/M&A rankings also assess individual lawyer leadership.

### Required information

- lead partner
- relationship partner
- specialist partners
- senior associates
- who negotiated what
- who handled client strategy

## Weakness checklist

RankPilot should flag a Corporate/M&A matter if it lacks:

- transaction type
- buyer/seller/client side
- firm role
- deal value or value band
- client profile
- strategic purpose
- complexity
- negotiation detail
- lawyer leadership
- market impact
- correct practice fit

## Recommended RankPilot output

When a matter is weak, RankPilot should distinguish between:

1. weak substance
2. weak narrative
3. wrong category
4. missing evidence
5. low strategic value

RankPilot should recommend whether the matter should be:

- rewritten
- expanded
- moved to another practice
- downgraded in order
- omitted
