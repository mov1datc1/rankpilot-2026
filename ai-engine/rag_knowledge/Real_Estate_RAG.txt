# Real Estate & Property Development — Chambers & Partners Intelligence Matrix

## 1. Practice Core & Editorial Identity
Real Estate in Chambers & Partners evaluates law firms that advise on:
- Major commercial, residential, industrial, and infrastructure real estate developments.
- Land-use, zoning, environmental permits, property rights, expropriation challenges, and administrative amparo litigation defending real estate assets.
- Real estate acquisition, leasing, concession management, and project continuity against government interventions.

## 2. National Ranking Positioning vs Regional Self-Limitation
- **National Recognition Requirement**: To achieve a national ranking in Mexico Real Estate, the submission must emphasize national geographic reach across multiple states (e.g. Jalisco, Guanajuato, Durango, Querétaro, CDMX) and federal administrative/constitutional tribunals.
- **B10/C2 Anti-Pattern**: NEVER describe the practice as purely local or regional (e.g. "operating primarily in Guadalajara"). Frame headquarters in Guadalajara as a strategic base serving national project developers across Mexico.

## 3. Hero Matter Strategy & Case Prioritization
- **Hero Matter Criteria**: The Hero Matter (#1 position in submission and B10 narrative) MUST be the case that best demonstrates real estate asset protection, high-stakes exposure, acquired property rights, and successful project continuity under government or environmental challenges.
- **Substantive Legal Reasoning**: Always preserve the exact legal mechanism (e.g. acquired rights under municipal zoning decrees, interim amparo relief protecting concession operational continuity, technical/scientific rebuttal of environmental restrictions).
- **Conciseness vs Lawyering**: NEVER compress away the causal legal mechanism that explains why the firm's work was exceptional. Outcomes show results; substantive legal criteria prove elite lawyering.

## 4. Wrong-Fit Practice Exclusion
- Pure corporate tax, routine labor inspection, or unrelated banking compliance matters do NOT belong in Section D/E of a Real Estate submission. Requisition or reposition peripheral matters to keep the portfolio concentrated on core real estate and public-law property protection.
