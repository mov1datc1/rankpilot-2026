# RankPilot editorial methodology — Tax
Scope: cross-jurisdictional evaluation method, not official directory rules or evidence about a firm.

## Understand the mandate before selecting
Reconstruct client identity, transaction or dispute, precise tax issue, legal work, result and current procedural status from the source. A transaction tax mandate must not become tax litigation. Do not infer an authority, tax, remedy or jurisdiction from the practice label. Domestic and international elements must be individually sourced.

## Comparative evidence
Compare technically distinctive tax advice, restructuring, transactions, controversy, compliance and private-client work on their demonstrated contribution to this candidature. Amount alone does not establish importance. Separate investment size, assessed tax, exposure, savings and fees; preserve currencies without conversion. Interim relief is not a final victory. Routine work can add a missing capability; do not fill a quota or exclude on labels alone.

## Jurisdiction and cross-border scope
Name authorities and jurisdictions only when the submission evidence supports them. A foreign institution can be legitimate in a cross-border mandate. Treaty advice, incorporation, affected assets and relevant jurisdictions must reconcile with the cross-border field. An unresolved factual contradiction requires source review; never replace authority names or force a field by client name.

## Individual and institutional strategy
Distinguish current source-declared ranking, official observation for a dated edition, and requested movement. Evaluate each person's attributed work, specialization and outcomes. Combine sourced current involvement with verified recognition without inventing leadership. A source-backed succession argument can strengthen the department narrative. Explain proposed categories and changes to supplied objectives. No guaranteed bands, market-dominance scores or unsupported claims about directory preferences.

## Writing and delivery
Use facts, legal work, outcomes and personal attribution to demonstrate differentiation. Each matter needs its own decisive evidence, not repeated generic continuity language. Missing decisive evidence becomes a precise question or a reasoned reserve decision, not invented detail. Audit and Submission consume the same identities, facts and portfolio decisions. Use separate voices: internal evaluation versus the firm's case for recognition. External comparative claims require dated official evidence; internal assessment alone is not market calibration.
