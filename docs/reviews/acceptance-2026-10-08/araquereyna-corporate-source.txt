ARAQUEREYNA’s Corporate and M&A practice offers comprehensive legal counsel across a wide range of corporate and transactional matters, including general corporate law, incorporation, governance, restructuring, mergers, acquisitions, divestitures, joint ventures, corporate and project finance, capital markets, real estate transactions, infrastructure, construction, domestic and international contracts, distribution agreements, foreign investment, exchange control, compliance, fintech, and e-commerce.

We work closely with our Banking, Tax, and Compliance teams to provide comprehensive solutions tailored to our clients’ specific needs.

E2 Summary of matter and your department's role – Please say why this matter was important. Also, tell us exactly what role your department played
ARAQUEREYNA's Corporate and M&A Department acted as legal counsel in a negotiation on behalf of the Spanish company Dragados, S.A. with the Attorney General's Office of the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela in relation to an advance payment made to Dragados in 1999 for the construction of the "Las Palmas" dam in the state of Cojedes in Venezuela. The Venezuelan government tacitly withdrew from the contract.  As a result of the withdrawal, the Republic of Venezuela was obliged to pay Dragados 16% of the value of the contract (US$60,000,000.00) under Venezuela's general rules on government contracts.

However, the Republic of Venezuela brought an action against Dragados before the Venezuelan Supreme Court, arguing that the contract provided for the jurisdiction of the Venezuelan courts under Venezuelan law. On the other hand, Dragados S.A. filed a lawsuit against the Republic of Venezuela in the courts of Madrid on the grounds of "forum non-conveniens" before the lawsuit was filed in Venezuela. 

ARAQUEREYNA's involvement consisted of negotiating and implementing a solution to the situation and designing a mechanism for compliance with the agreements with the Attorney General of the Republic, which led to the signing of a settlement agreement that put an end to the entire dispute. To implement the settlement, it was necessary to design and carry out transfers and banking operations for currency exchange, payment and the execution of the final contract, with the necessary waivers and settlements, both in the Courts of Madrid, Spain, and in the Supreme Court of Venezuela. 

As a result of the team's professional intervention and coordination, both parties were satisfied, and the entire dispute was finally resolved in relation to a construction contract originally valued at US $60,000,000.00.
