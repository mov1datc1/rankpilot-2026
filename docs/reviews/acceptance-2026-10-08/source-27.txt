EL CIELO COUNTRY CLUB — a high-end residential development at Cerro de Bugambilias combining urban development with environmental conservation.
D2 Summary of matter and your department's role – Please say why this matter was important. Also, tell us exactly what role your department played.
Administrative decrees issued by the Government of the State of Jalisco and the Municipality of Tlajomulco de Zúñiga designated El Cielo Country Club—a first-class development on the Bugambilias hill integrating urbanisation with nature—as a Natural Protected Area, halting its urbanisation process and placing MXN 3.000.000.000,00 (Approx USD 172,37,026.00) in deployed development value at risk. The measures threatened the continuity, patrimony, market position and subsistence of the development, as well as the property interests of purchasers who had invested in the project. The team responded through amparo proceedings filed in 2018 and 2021, converting constitutional review into protection for the development’s existing land-use rights, housing permits and environmental credentials. In the 2018 proceeding, it established the legality of the development, the continuing validity of permits granted before the decrees, and the finding that the project was environmentally friendly and sustainable rather than environmentally harmful. The Sixth Collegiate Administrative Court of the Third Circuit, in case file 347/2022, subsequently upheld the amparo granted at first instance. In the 2021 proceeding, the team secured nullification of the decree updating the Local Ecological Management Program for the Municipality of Tlajomulco de Zúñiga insofar as it affected the client’s properties; that judgment was duly enforced in July 2024. The outcome preserved the commercial viability of El Cielo Country Club and reinforced legal certainty for urban-development investment by confirming that political or administrative decisions cannot disregard authorised land uses already under execution. It also established the importance of technically and scientifically grounded environmental measures where established developments and purchaser interests are at stake. José Pablo Ramos Castillo led the representation, with Daniel Rocha Peña and Hector Alejandro Sánchez Carrera as team members.

DURANPARK, S.A. DE C.V. — developer of the Durango Logistics and Industrial Center.
D2 Summary of matter and your department's role – Please say why this matter was important. Also, tell us exactly what role your department played.
DURANPARK, S.A. DE C.V., a construction-sector company engaged in civil works, urban development, project design and supervision, faces the potential loss of property and possession rights over the Durango Logistics and Industrial Center, a 207.5306-hectare asset with MXN $698,400,750.00 at stake. The exposure arose after the Public Registry of Property and Commerce of the State of Durango refused registration of the transfer formalised through public deed 23,134 of June 8, 2022, following Decree CCXXXIX, through which the State of Durango expropriated the relevant properties. The disputed asset had been approved for transfer under agreement 198/2022 of February 24, 2022, involving Trust No. 851-01075, placing Duranpark’s deployed investment, operational continuity and acquired property rights at material risk. The team conducted a detailed assessment of Duranpark’s contractual and documentary position vis-à-vis the State authorities, including the Constitutional Governor of the State of Durango, the State Congress, the Ministry of Social Welfare, the Ministry of Finance and Administration, the Secretary General of Government, the Director of the Official State Newspaper of Durango and the Public Registry. It translated the client’s acquired-rights and due-process protections into an indirect amparo challenge to the constitutionality of the February 4, 2024 Expropriation Decree, supported by the underlying transfer, trust and registration record. A District Court granted a definitive suspension preventing acts affecting the property or possession of the 207.5306 hectares, as well as administrative or registration measures connected with the expropriation, while the constitutional hearing remains pending. The definitive suspension safeguards Duranpark’s ability to preserve the industrial and logistics asset pending final constitutional review, limiting the immediate commercial effects of the expropriation and registration refusal. A finding that Decree CCXXXIX is unconstitutional would provide a significant reference point for comparable challenges involving expropriation measures affecting previously acquired property rights. José Pablo Ramos Castillo leads the mandate, with Daniel Rocha Peña and Héctor Alejandro Sánchez Carrera supporting the constitutional challenge, documentary analysis and protective strategy.

IDEX — developer of Brasilia, a major mixed-use residential and commercial project in Guadalajara.
D2 Summary of matter and your department's role – Please say why this matter was important. Also, tell us exactly what role your department played.
IDEX, among its various projects, initiated the commercially denominated "Brasilia", located in the city of Guadalajara, Jalisco, consisting of a vertical development of 328 housing units and 20 commercial units. This development was the object of a serious attack by neighbors, neighborhood associations and the State Urban Development Attorney's Office (PRODEUR), who launched media, legal and jurisdictional attacks, alleging that "Brasilia" affected the correct urban development of the city, since it allegedly had a negative impact on traffic, concentration of inhabitants, water and electricity services and the enjoyment of the city, in addition to claiming alleged damage to groundwater. The aforementioned, without having any real legal basis, but simply the pretension of obtaining benefits through collective pressure. These organizations sought the closure, obstruction and non-execution of Brasilia by filing several injunction lawsuits, requesting suspensions and exerting pressure through the authorities. As a result, the development was the victim of four simultaneous suspensions that ordered the closure of the development, based on alleged environmental impacts and risks. These suspensions were the result of four different lawsuits that ordered the closure of the project to prevent its execution. Our team of lawyers managed to revoke the four suspensions in a period of 3 weeks, generating important legal criteria, such as: (i) the environmental impact must be sufficiently accredited as something real, actual and imminent and not only as a mere assumption without basis; (ii) although there is a Supreme Court criterion to immediately halt developments when an environmental impact is alleged, we achieved its application in a balanced manner, leaving established that whoever claims an environmental impact or risk must do so with sufficient grounds and not based on allegations without technical support. Our firm was able to establish a very important legal precedent, even though the Supreme Court of Justice of Mexico ruled against it. As a result, developers now have guarantees regarding the continuity of their operations. This case evidenced a clear opportunity to defend against generic or unfounded allegations of environmental damage.
