Ramos Castillo Abogados is recognised for its highly specialised real estate-facing administrative, public law, tax and amparo capability, particularly where government action, regulatory decisions or public-sector participation place a client’s assets and operations under significant exposure. The team combines personalised advice with a litigation-led approach to resolving complex disputes comprehensively, serving companies active across the energy, construction and real estate-services sectors. Its work is rooted in long-term client relationships and in supporting the growth and strategic development of projects whose viability depends on a strong understanding of administrative law and effective constitutional defence.

A defining mandate is the firm’s representation of Inmobiliaria y Desarrollos La Primavera, S.A. de C.V. in proceedings against the Government of the State of Jalisco and the Municipality of Zapopan concerning the alleged appropriation, without prior compensation, of property belonging to the client. Through expert real-estate identification evidence, the team demonstrated that the State Government had unjustifiably entered private property, as the asset was not located in the place or area stipulated in the relevant agreement. This matter illustrates the firm’s ability to address disputes at the intersection of real estate ownership, public authority and evidential complexity. Its portfolio also includes representation of NEXT Energy de Mexico, S.A. de C.V. in connection with the Energy Regulatory Commission’s refusal to grant an electric-power generation permit for a photovoltaic plant in Baja California. The project contemplated 300.532 MW DC of installed capacity, an interconnection-point capacity of 267.575 MW AC and supply of 754,892 MWh.

Founding partner José Pablo Ramos Castillo leads the practice alongside associate attorneys Juan Carlos de Obeso Orendain, Edgar Adrián Moro López, Mónica Dariane Cárdenas Fregoso, Cecilia Cortés Diaz Corona, Hector Alejandro Sánchez Carrera, Daniel Rocha Peña and Sara Elena Vizcaino Sedano. Working closely as a team, they provide legal defence in administrative and amparo proceedings for corporate clients, including HOLCIM México Operaciones, S.A. de C.V. Inmobiliaria Desarrollo La Primavera, S.A. de C.V. IDEX - Brasilia and Grupo R - Concesión Zapopan.

The practice’s principal base is Guadalajara and the State of Jalisco, where many of its clients operate, while its Baja California energy mandate evidences its ability to act beyond its core regional market. Ramos Castillo Abogados has also established jurisdictional and administrative precedents through matters involving government participation, helping to safeguard the operational continuity of companies and contributing to the region’s economic development.

Approximately 35 challenges brought by unsuccessful bidders and third parties threatened to paralyse or revoke a concession with public-order, social-interest and social-utility implications, as well as GRUPO R’s positioning as a leading energy company in the State of Jalisco.

D2 Summary of matter and your department's role – Please say why this matter was important. Also, tell us exactly what role your department played.
The MXN 1.300.000.000,00 (approximately USD 74,747,252.26) Brasilia development in Guadalajara, Jalisco—a vertical project comprising 328 housing units and 20 commercial units—faced a coordinated challenge that threatened its construction, sale and scheduled delivery. Neighbours, neighbourhood associations and the State Urban Development Attorney’s Office (PRODEUR) alleged adverse effects on traffic, population concentration, water and electricity services, enjoyment of the city and groundwater, pursuing media, administrative and judicial pressure to halt the project.

The defence required the firm to address four simultaneous lawsuits and suspensions ordering closure of the development on alleged environmental-impact and risk grounds. The team challenged the absence of technical and legal support for the allegations, securing a balanced application of the Supreme Court of Justice of Mexico criterion concerning the immediate suspension of developments where environmental harm is alleged. Its strategy focused on demonstrating that environmental impact must be substantiated as real, current and imminent, rather than resting on unverified assumptions.

In early 2020, the team obtained revocation of all four suspensions within three weeks and successfully dismissed the four lawsuits, confirming the full legality of Brasilia and enabling construction, sales and delivery to proceed according to schedule. The matter established an important precedent that parties alleging environmental impact or risk must support their claims with sufficient technical grounds, providing developers with greater protection against generic or unfounded environmental allegations. José Pablo Ramos Castillo led the matter, with Daniel Peña Rocha and Hector Alejandro Sanchez Carrera actively supporting the litigation and suspension-revocation strategy.
