Transformation Summary
The blueprint establishes a property-rights and development-continuity narrative rather than treating the record as a general compilation of administrative and tax disputes. El Cielo leads because it joins development permissions, ecological restrictions, reported upheld relief and 2024 enforcement. Duranpark, Brasilia, Diageo, administrative recovery, San Pedro Tlaquepaque and property-classification matters then demonstrate recurrence across residential, industrial, logistics, environmental and individual-property contexts. Public procurement, tax, VAT, labour-inspection, freight and fuel-regulation matters remain fully preserved and are repositioned as contextual administrative or tax evidence. Only two confidential transport tax-and-labour entries are narratively de-emphasised because the supplied facts materially overlap; neither is removed. No Type B evidence—values, dates, hectare counts, unit counts, case numbers, team names, procedural stages or confidentiality status—is to be condensed or omitted in the eventual document.
