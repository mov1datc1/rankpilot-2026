Matter description
DeForest advises Schaeffler, a major German automotive and industrial-components group, on the labour integration of Vitesco Technologies’ Mexican operations following Schaeffler’s acquisition of the company. The transaction created a significant post-acquisition workforce integration exercise spanning manufacturing facilities across Puebla, the State of Mexico, Guanajuato and Jalisco and affecting more than 5,000 employees.
Led by Eduardo Garduño, DeForest assumed responsibility for harmonising the employment structures inherited from the two businesses into a coherent labour framework. The team has standardised individual employment agreements and internal work regulations, aligned collective bargaining arrangements, unified mandatory mixed commissions and company-wide employment policies, and ensured consistent implementation of Mexico’s labour-reform requirements across the combined operation.
The mandate is particularly significant because the integration extends beyond documentation and compliance into the management of legacy employment risk. In parallel with the harmonisation process, DeForest manages 35 active labour claims involving former employees and suppliers, ensuring that the litigation strategy is consistent with the newly integrated corporate and workforce structure.
The project has progressively expanded to additional Mexican facilities, including the Santa Anita plant in Jalisco, making DeForest a continuing labour adviser to the combined operation rather than counsel solely for the transaction itself. The mandate demonstrates the team’s ability to manage the employment consequences of a major corporate acquisition across multiple industrial sites while maintaining workforce and operational continuity.
Lead partner: Eduardo Garduño — Puebla Other key team member: Ana Ruiz — Puebla Status: Ongoing Confidential / non-publishable: Yes, según el submission de Legal 500.

8. Nueva Empresa — Javier Atzin Vallejo
Este asunto me gusta más cuanto más lo miro porque no es “otro wrongful dismissal”. El despido es sólo el vehículo; el asunto real es constitucional/procesal.
DeForest represents Nueva Empresa, a Mexican private-education company, in two wrongful termination proceedings brought by long-tenured former employees, with aggregate exposure of approximately MXN3 million. The disputes developed into an unusual constitutional issue concerning the legal identity of the company’s female attorney-in-fact and the use of her maiden and married names at different stages of her life.
Although DeForest had duly responded to both claims and submitted notarial evidence recognising the attorney-in-fact under multiple legally valid names, the labour court refused to recognise her standing, treated the claims as unanswered and issued an adverse ruling ordering payment of statutory employment benefits.
Led by Javier Atzin Vallejo, DeForest challenged the decision through amparo proceedings, arguing that the court’s treatment of the attorney-in-fact’s identity violated due process and fundamental rights. The Collegiate Court granted constitutional relief, ordered the labour court to recognise her legal standing and admitted the company’s defences.
Beyond reversing a decision carrying approximately MXN3 million in exposure, the matter is significant because it addresses the legal consequences arising where a woman has been formally identified under different legally recognised names over the course of her life, bringing questions of identity and fundamental rights into employment proceedings.
